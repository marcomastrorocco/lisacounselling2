Domestic & Family Violence, Coercive Control and Abuse

Abuse is not limited to physical violence — and its effects can continue long after the situation has ended. Whether you are still in the relationship, have recently left, or are carrying experiences from many years ago, specialised, trauma-informed support is available. You do not need to have all the answers before seeking help.

You deserve to feel safe, respected and in control of your own life.

Understanding domestic and family violence

Domestic and family violence can involve a pattern of behaviours used to gain power and control: coercive control, emotional and psychological abuse, physical or sexual violence, financial abuse, intimidation and stalking, isolation from support networks, technology-facilitated abuse, and ongoing post-separation abuse. Many people minimise or question their experiences — “Was it really abuse?” — particularly when there were no visible injuries. These responses are common and can develop when fear, control or uncertainty have become part of everyday life.

The lasting impact

Experiences of abuse can affect emotional wellbeing, relationships and your sense of safety. Anxiety, hypervigilance, difficulty trusting, shame, emotional numbness, sleep difficulties, depression and grief are not signs of weakness — they are understandable responses to frightening and overwhelming experiences.

How I can support you

In a safe, confidential and non-judgemental space, we can work to strengthen emotional safety and stability, develop coping strategies, rebuild confidence and self-worth, explore healthy boundaries, process grief and loss, support parenting after violence, and reconnect with your strengths and goals. You are always in control of what you choose to share, and there is never pressure to talk about anything before you feel ready.
