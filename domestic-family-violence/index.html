Domestic & Family Violence, Coercive Control and Abuse

Abuse is not limited to physical violence — and its effects can continue long after the situation has ended. Whether you are still in the relationship, have recently left, or are carrying experiences from many years ago, specialised, trauma-informed support is available. You do not need to have all the answers before seeking help.

You deserve to feel safe, respected and in control of your own life.

Understanding domestic and family violence

Domestic and family violence can involve a pattern of behaviours used to gain power and control: coercive control, emotional and psychological abuse, physical or sexual violence, financial abuse, intimidation and stalking, isolation from support networks, technology-facilitated abuse, and ongoing post-separation abuse. Many people minimise or question their experiences — “Was it really abuse?” — particularly when there were no visible injuries. These responses are common, and often reflect what it means to live where fear or control became part of everyday life.

The lasting impact

Abuse affects the nervous system, relationships and wellbeing. Anxiety, hypervigilance, difficulty trusting, shame, emotional numbness, sleep difficulties, depression and grief are not signs of weakness — they are understandable responses to frightening and overwhelming experiences.

How I can support you

In a safe, confidential and non-judgemental space, we can work to strengthen emotional safety and stability, develop grounding strategies, rebuild confidence and self-worth, explore healthy boundaries, process grief and loss, support parenting after violence, and reconnect with your strengths and goals. There is never pressure to share more than feels safe.

My experience

I bring more than 15 years of experience across Queensland Government, health and community services supporting women, men, young people and families affected by domestic and family violence, coercive control, childhood abuse, sexual violence and violent crime — including crisis intervention, risk assessment and safety planning.

A safe place to begin

Recovery is not about forgetting what happened. It is about understanding its impact, rebuilding your sense of safety, and creating the life you want to live. Healing is possible, and support is available when you are ready.
